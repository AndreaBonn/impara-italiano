## What you need

A browser from 2020 or later. That is the whole list.

There is no account to create, no password, no email address and no subscription. The course does not know who you are, and it never asks.

Two features depend on the browser rather than on the course:

| Feature | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Speech recognition (speaking exercises, conversations) | Chrome, Edge or Safari 16+, on `https://` or `localhost` |
| Offline use and installing as an app | Any page served over `http(s)`, not opened from disk |

Everything else, including all the recorded narration, works anywhere, including from a file on your own disk.

## First run

The first screen asks one question: where do you want to begin.

- **I am starting from scratch** takes you to lesson one of A1. Nothing needs configuring first.
- **I already know some** opens the placement test. It jumps between levels instead of walking up from A1, so it finishes in a few minutes even if you know a lot. About thirty items across three rounds.
- **Let me look around first** opens the learning path and lets you decide later.

The placement test ends with a proposal, not a verdict. If it says B1, accepting marks the lessons below B1 as done and drops you at the start of B1. You can turn it down and begin at A1 anyway, and you can retake the test at any point from Settings. Lessons ticked off this way earn no points, because you did not do them.

**Choosing your explanation language.** The Italian is always Italian; the explanations around it come in Polish, English, Spanish, French or German. The picker sits at the top of the sidebar. Switching is instant and costs you nothing: your progress, your flashcards and your mistakes are keyed to the Italian, not to the translation.

Contrastive notes are written separately for each language rather than translated, because the traps differ. The note that warns a Polish speaker about grammatical gender says something else entirely to an American one.

## How the course is organized

Six CEFR levels, in order, with cumulative grammar. A2 assumes A1 is behind you and B1 assumes A2. Nothing stops you jumping ahead, but from that point the lessons stop explaining themselves.

| Level | Italian name | The grammar it adds |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Contatto | present tense, articles, gender and number, reflexives |
| A2 | Sopravvivenza | imperfetto, futuro, object pronouns, imperative |
| B1 | Soglia | congiuntivo, combined pronouns, relative clauses, reported speech |
| B2 | Progresso | congiuntivo imperfetto, conditionals, passive, passato remoto |
| C1 | Efficacia | the values of *si*, pronominal verbs, register, text cohesion |
| C2 | Padronanza | participles, periphrases, difficult plurals, irony, latinisms |

Inside a level the units are ordered, and so are the lessons inside a unit. Each unit ends with a test covering the whole unit, not just the last lesson.

**The pass mark is 70 percent.** Below that the lesson stays open and you can take it again from scratch, with the score reset. Nothing is lost by failing.

## Inside a lesson

Every lesson has the same six parts, in this order.

1. **Objectives.** What you will be able to do by the end.
2. **Theory.** The explanation, in your language. It stays on the page while you work, so you can scroll back to it mid-exercise. Watch for the three marked boxes: *Watch out* (a common mistake), *If you speak English* (where Italian works differently from your language), *Tip*.
3. **Grammar.** A table plus examples. Every Italian example has a speaker button.
4. **Vocabulary.** The new words, each with its recording. The star next to a word adds it to your review deck. **Add all to review** takes the whole list at once, and **Play the whole list** reads it aloud.
5. **Dialogue.** A short scene with two voices. **Play the whole dialogue** runs it end to end.
6. **Through Italian eyes.** A culture note. Nothing to answer.
7. **Exercises.** Between five and fifteen, mixed types.

At the end you get a score and one of four messages. Above 70 percent the lesson is marked done and the next one is offered. Below it, you are pointed back at the theory.

**Every wrong answer opens a card in your mistake notebook.** Not a similar exercise: that exact one, tagged with the grammar topic it belongs to. It comes back under Review. There is nothing for you to write down or mark.

## The exercise types

Fourteen types. All of them tell you the right answer when you get it wrong, and none of them let you move on without answering.

| Type | What you do |
|---|---|
| Multiple choice | pick one of the options |
| Pick several | select every correct answer, not just one |
| Fill the gap | type the missing word |
| Fill the text | type into several numbered gaps in one passage |
| Translation | write the whole sentence, in Italian or in your language |
| Build the sentence | click word tiles into the right order |
| Matching | pair Italian phrases with their meanings |
| Conjugation | write all six persons of a verb in a given tense |
| Articles | choose the right article for a noun |
| True / false | judge a statement about a text |
| Listening | hear a sentence and write it down |
| Pronunciation | say the sentence out loud into the microphone |
| Conversation | reply inside a short scripted dialogue |
| Minimal pair | hear one of two near-identical words and say which |

Two of these behave differently from the rest.

**Listening** gives you a play button and a slower button. There is no transcript until you have answered.

**Pronunciation** needs the microphone. Without it, or without your consent, it turns into a written exercise: listen to the model, repeat it aloud yourself, then type the sentence from memory and mark it done. You lose the automatic check, not the exercise.

Near-misses are handled kindly. A typo or a missing accent gets *Almost! Check the spelling* rather than a flat rejection, so you can see what you actually got wrong.

## Typing Italian

Italian accents are not on a Polish or an American keyboard, and the course does not punish you for your keyboard layout.

**The character bar.** Focus any text field in an exercise and a small bar appears just above it with `à è é ì ò ù` and the apostrophe. Click a character and it lands at the cursor. The bar sits above the field rather than below so it cannot cover the check button.

**Keyboard shortcuts.** These work when the cursor is not inside a text field:

| Key | Action |
|---|---|
| `Enter` | move to the next exercise, whenever a next button is waiting |
| `1` to `9` | pick the corresponding answer option |

`Enter` also works from inside a text field, because by then the exercise is already checked and locked.

**Accent strictness.** Settings has a switch called *Require accents*. Off, `perche` is accepted for `perché`. On, it is not. Turning it on is worth it once you are past A1: the accents change the pronunciation, and the recordings will teach you the difference whether you write it or not.

## Sound

The Italian you hear is recorded by a neural narrator, not synthesized by your browser. Two voices: Isabella narrates, Giuseppe plays the other speaker in dialogues. This is why the course sounds the same on every operating system.

Settings controls three things about it:

- **Voice source.** Recorded narration (the default) or your system voice. There is no reason to pick the system voice except to check what it sounds like.
- **Speech rate.** In recorded mode this changes playback speed, and Chrome preserves pitch, so a slowed sentence still sounds like a person.
- **Play recordings automatically in exercises.** On by default.

Two places fall back to the system voice on purpose. **Conjugated forms** in the verb conjugator are generated on demand, so they cannot have been recorded in advance. And any sentence you add yourself has no recording until one is built.

If your system has no Italian voice at all, the course says so and stays silent rather than reading Italian in an English accent, which would teach you the wrong pronunciation. The recordings are unaffected by this.

## The microphone and your voice

This is the one part of the course where something leaves your device.

Speech recognition does not run on your computer. The browsers that offer it send a recording of your voice to their own vendor's server and send text back. The course has no control over that and cannot do it differently without a server of its own, which it deliberately does not have.

Because of that, the first time anything would use speech recognition the course stops and asks. The gate sits in one place in the code, so nothing can slip past it.

- **Agree**, and the pronunciation exercises, the spoken conversations and the exam speaking section work as designed.
- **Decline**, and those exercises become written ones. Nothing else changes, and the rest of the course is unaffected.

You can revoke the decision later in Settings. It affects the roughly 150 pronunciation exercises and the fourteen conversations, and nothing else: theory, written exercises, listening, flashcards and progress never leave your browser.

**Shadowing and the exam speaking section are different.** Those record you with the microphone but do not send anything anywhere: the audio stays in the page and is gone when you close it. They are for listening to yourself, not for being graded.

## The screens, one by one

Sixteen entries in the sidebar. None of them is mandatory.

### Today

A session assembled for you, for a day when you do not want to choose. It always has three parts in this order: exercises you got wrong, a drill on whatever topic is going worst, then the flashcards that are due.

The session never comes up empty, even on a day when you are behind on nothing, because the drills are generated rather than drawn from a list. Ten minutes is the intended size. Doing a second one breaks nothing.

### Learning path

Levels, units and lessons. This is where the actual studying happens, and where **Pick up where you left off** takes you back to the last thing you touched.

Levels load on demand, so the first click on a new level takes a moment.

### Review

Two decks under two tabs, with two different rhythms.

**Cards** are the words you starred yourself. They run on FSRS: a word you know comes back less and less often, a word you keep tripping on comes back tomorrow. The interval is computed from how you answer, not from the calendar. You grade yourself on four buttons: *No idea*, *With effort*, *Got it*, *Easy*. Grade honestly; that grade is the only input the schedule has.

**Mistakes** is the notebook, and it fills itself. It holds the exercises you got wrong, each labelled with its grammar topic, so you see what went wrong and which rule it belonged to. Two correct answers in a row and a card retires. If an exercise changes in the course, its card is dropped and you are told how many went.

The badge on the Review tab counts both decks. Zero means nothing is due today, not that you are finished.

**How often cards come back** is adjustable in Settings, in three steps. Less often means fewer reviews a day and more words lost; more often is the other way around. The change applies from your next answer, and dates already set stay where they are.

### Training

Exercises built from rules rather than drawn from a list, so they do not run out after four rounds. Seven topics:

Preposition plus article, adjective agreement, essere or avere, object pronouns, numbers, dates, telling the time.

Every round draws new items, and anything you get wrong lands in the Mistakes tab. Training tasks are always written, never spoken: a generated sentence has no recording, so there is nothing to play.

Three further screens hang off Training, because they train the ear and the hand rather than a rule: [Minimal pairs](#minimal-pairs), [Reading and listening](#reading-and-listening) and [Writing](#writing).

### Speaking practice

Fourteen conversations in real situations: the bar, introductions, the market, a restaurant, the station, the doctor, renting a flat, a job interview, an office, an argument at the dinner table. The course speaks Italian; you answer into the microphone, or by typing if you would rather.

Four of them fork. What you say changes what happens next, and once you reach the end you can go back to the choice and play the other branch.

If you are stuck, **Show the answer** reveals the model. Without speech recognition the whole screen works in typing mode.

### Grammar

The full A1 to C2 syllabus, 42 entries, arranged by topic and meant for reading rather than answering. Come here when something in a lesson needs a wider explanation than a lesson can carry.

### Verb conjugator

Type any infinitive, including reflexives like `svegliarsi`, and get fourteen tenses and moods. The engine handles regular verbs plus about 45 of the commonest irregulars, and it applies the spelling changes (`-care`, `-gare`, `-ciare`, `-giare`, `-iare`) rather than pretending they do not exist.

Reflexives in the imperative are done properly: the pronoun attaches for tu, noi and voi (`alzati`, `alziamoci`, `alzatevi`) and stands before the polite form (`si alzi`).

Forms here are read by the system voice, because they are generated on the spot and cannot have been recorded.

### My vocabulary

Every word you starred, sorted by when it is next due. Three counters at the top: cards in the deck, learned (three or more reviews), due today. There is a search box, and a Remove button on each entry.

The deck starts empty. A star next to any word in any lesson fills it.

### Shadowing

Listen to a sentence, say it back, then play both takes one after the other and hear the difference yourself. Nothing is scored and nothing is sent anywhere: your recordings live in the page and are gone when you close it.

This one needs microphone permission but not speech recognition, so it works in browsers that have no recognition at all. Without a microphone the listening half still works.

### Speed

The same sentence at native speed. There is a slower button, and it is a lifeline rather than a starting point: nobody outside a classroom speaks slow Italian. Afterwards the screen tells you whether you reached for it.

Show the sentence reveals the text once you have tried.

### Exam

A CILS B1 Cittadinanza simulation. Sections run in a fixed order, each with its own timer, and answers go into a grid instead of being checked one at a time. The instructions are in Italian, because on the real exam they will be.

Four skills, six tests: two listening (30 minutes), two reading with grammar (40 minutes), a written text of 80 to 120 words (40 minutes) and a conversation (about 10 minutes). Listening texts can be played twice, as in the real thing.

**Two of the four skills are actually scored.** Listening and reading produce a number. The written task comes back as a list of which parts of the brief you covered and which you missed. The speaking part is not scored at all: it records you and gives you a checklist to go through yourself. Those two are marked by a human in the real exam, and an automatic number would look like a grade without being one.

The result page therefore has three possible verdicts, and one of them is *undecided*, honestly labelled as such.

There is no point trying this early. It measures a level you already have; it is not a way to reach one. The sensible moment is the end of the level you are sitting for.

### False friends

Words that mean something other than your language suggests. The list is written per language rather than translated, because the trap depends on what you already carry: `parente` traps an English speaker, and a different set traps a Polish one.

The list is browsable, and **Test yourself** runs a ten-word quiz where every wrong option is exactly the trap in question.

### Coverage

How much of real Italian you already hold. The screen compares your deck against the most frequent Italian word forms and shows two numbers: what your deck covers, and the ceiling the course itself reaches.

Inflection counts. Having `bere` covers `bevo`, because the screen lemmatizes the forms rather than matching strings.

Below the numbers, **Closest gaps** lists the most frequent words you do not have yet and the course does teach, each with the forms it appears in, and an Add button.

The frequency list comes from the Tatoeba corpus, and the screen names its source, sentence count and license at the bottom.

### Progress

Streak, best streak, lessons completed, answer accuracy, points, a chart of the last two weeks and a per-level breakdown. All counted locally, in your browser.

### How to use it

A shorter version of this manual, inside the course itself.

### Settings

| Group | What is in it |
|---|---|
| Speech | voice source, speech rate, autoplay, require accents, a voice test button |
| Reviews | how often cards come back, in three steps |
| Progress backup | export, import, erase everything |
| Browser support | which of TTS, speech recognition and offline mode work here, and which Italian voices your system has |

Settings is also where the placement test lives if you want to retake it, and where the Anki deck export and import sit.

**Anki export** writes your words and translations in a format Anki, a spreadsheet and every other flashcard program can read. Due dates do not travel: the words leave, the calendar does not, and in Anki the cards start from zero. **Anki import** shows you what it will add, update and skip before it touches anything.

## Screens without a menu entry

Four screens are reachable but not in the sidebar.

### Minimal pairs

Two words that differ by a single sound. You hear one and say which it was. Reached from Training.

This is the check that comes before pronunciation work: until the ear catches a difference, the mouth will not make it. Sets where the recordings turned out to be indistinguishable are left out of the course rather than shipped, so every pair here is genuinely tellable apart.

### Reading and listening

Twelve longer texts, each in three modes: **Read**, **Listen** (the text is hidden, so you cannot read along) and **Dictation** (sentence by sentence). Questions follow, and there is a glossary of the harder words.

Tap any word in a text and a card tells you what it means and offers to add it to your reviews. If the course does not know the word, the card says so rather than guessing, and lets you type your own meaning. Reached from Training.

### Writing

Six tasks, in two kinds: compositions and sentence-by-sentence translation. Reached from Training.

The screen is honest about its own limits. Nothing here grades your prose. What gets checked is what can be checked: whether you used the constructions the task asked for, and whether you hit the word count. Then it shows you a model text, not as a template to copy but to compare against, and a list of questions to ask yourself, ideally after reading your own text out loud.

### Search

The magnifying glass above the sidebar. It searches lesson titles, vocabulary, grammar entries and conversations, in Italian or in your language, with accents or without. Two characters minimum.

## Your progress and how to keep it

Everything lives in your browser's storage, on this device. There is no account and no sync, which means two things.

**Clearing your browsing data erases it for good.** Not recoverable, not rebuildable, gone.

**A file is the only backup there is.** Settings has an Export button that downloads one, and an Import button that loads it back. Import is also the only way to move progress to another computer.

The course reminds you. Every ten finished lessons a message appears with a download button, and it stays on screen until you deal with it rather than fading away. Dismissing it is not the same as saving: the reminder comes back next time. Only an actual download resets the counter.

If browser storage fills up, the course tells you and starts protecting what matters. It drops the best-learned review cards first, then drill counters. Lesson progress and your written compositions are never dropped, because those are the things further study will not rebuild.

## Working offline and installing the course

Served over `http(s)`, the course installs a service worker on the first visit and works without a network from then on. Recordings join the cache as you listen to them, so offline coverage grows with use rather than downloading 45 MB up front.

Settings shows which of three states you are in: working, still setting up, or unavailable because the page was opened from disk.

**Installing it as an app.** In Chrome or Edge, the install icon in the address bar. On iOS Safari, Share then Add to Home Screen. It then opens in its own window with no browser chrome, and the offline behavior is the same.

**Opened from a file on disk** (`file://`), the course still runs: all the lessons, all the recordings, all the exercises. The browser refuses to register a service worker there, so nothing is kept for later, and speech recognition needs a secure context it does not have.

## Updates

New versions announce themselves rather than installing behind your back. When one is ready you get a message with an **Update now** button; updating takes a moment and leaves your progress and cards untouched.

Closing the message with the cross keeps the new version waiting, and the message comes back next time you open the course. It only appears on a course that was already running, so a first visit never asks you to refresh a page you just opened.

## Study routines

**A day when you have ten minutes.** Open Today. It has already decided what you need.

**A day when you have an hour.** One new lesson from the path, then Review to clear the badge, then a Training round on whatever the lesson made you feel shaky about.

**A lesson that will not sink in.** Do not take it a third time in a row. Do a Training round on that topic instead, where the exercises are generated and different every time, and come back to the lesson the next day.

**Pronunciation that will not come.** Minimal pairs first, to check you hear the difference at all. Then Shadowing, to hear your own voice next to the narrator's. A rule will not help with this and neither will more exercises.

**Listening that falls apart on real speech.** Speed, and resist the slow button. Then the Listen mode of a reading text, which forces you to hold the sense across several sentences instead of one.

**Before an exam.** The exam simulation at the end of the level you are sitting, not before. Read what it does and does not score first.

## Troubleshooting

| What you see | What is happening |
|---|---|
| Nothing plays, sentences are silent | Your system has no Italian voice and the recordings are unavailable. The course stays silent rather than reading Italian in the wrong accent. Serve the page over `http(s)` so recordings can load. |
| A recording refuses to play | The sentence is in the index but the file is missing. Check that the `audio/` folder sits next to `index.html` and is served from the same place. |
| Pronunciation exercises are written instead of spoken | Either your browser has no speech recognition (needs Chrome, Edge or Safari 16+), or the page is not on `https`/`localhost`, or you declined the consent. Settings shows which. |
| The microphone button is missing entirely | No microphone was found, or the browser does not offer microphone access to pages. |
| Microphone permission was refused | Grant it from the address bar, then reload. |
| Speech recognition keeps rejecting correct Italian | It is harsh on a foreign accent. Listen to the model and try again at a calm pace. If it stays stubborn, use the typing fallback and mark the exercise done. |
| Offline mode says it needs a server | You opened the course from disk. Browsers do not allow service workers on `file://`. |
| Progress disappeared | Browsing data was cleared, or you are on a different browser or device. Import your backup file from Settings. |
| A message says storage is full | Export your progress from Settings and free up space. The course has already started dropping the safest things to protect your lessons. |
| An import is rejected | The message names the reason: not a save file, no version number, from a newer version of the course, a damaged field, or too large. |
| Part of the course stays in the previous language after switching | Some overlay files failed to load. The course says how many. Reload the page. |
| An exercise says "Unknown exercise type" | A script did not load. Reload; if it persists, it is a bug worth reporting. |
